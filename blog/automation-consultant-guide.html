What Does an Automation Consultant Actually Do?

7 min read · Working With a Consultant

"Automation consultant" is a fairly new job title, and it's reasonable to not be entirely sure what one actually does day-to-day, or how they differ from a software developer. Here's a straightforward answer.

The core job: translating your process into a working system

An automation consultant's main skill isn't really the technology — it's understanding a business process well enough to spot what's repetitive, rule-based, and worth automating, then building a reliable system that handles it. The technical building is often the easier part; correctly understanding what a business actually needs is the harder, more valuable skill.

How this is different from hiring a developer

A software developer typically builds custom applications from scratch — a bigger, more expensive undertaking. An automation consultant usually works with existing tools (your CRM, email platform, spreadsheets) and connects them together using automation platforms, which is faster, cheaper, and doesn't require building new software. For most small business problems, this is a more practical and affordable route than commissioning custom development.

What a typical project looks like

  1. Discovery — a conversation about your current process, where time is actually being lost, and what the ideal outcome looks like.
  2. Design — mapping out exactly what the automation should do, including how it should handle unusual or messy cases, not just the ideal scenario.
  3. Build & test — building the automation and testing it thoroughly before it touches real business data.
  4. Handover & support — the automation goes live, with ongoing monitoring so it keeps working reliably as your tools or processes change.

What to look for when choosing one

  • They ask about your process before pitching a solution. A good consultant wants to understand the problem thoroughly before suggesting a fix — be cautious of anyone proposing a solution before genuinely understanding what you need.
  • They explain things in plain English. You shouldn't need to understand the technology to understand what's being built and why.
  • They offer ongoing support, not just a one-off build. Automations need occasional maintenance — the tools they connect to change over time, and a good consultant accounts for that.
  • They're upfront about limitations. Not everything should be automated, and a trustworthy consultant will tell you when something isn't worth the investment.
